The problem

The prototype was quick. The last mile isn't.

AI tools get you a long way in a single evening. Then comes everything that separates a demo from an app in the store.

01

Fixes that break other things

The AI fixes one bug and creates a new one somewhere else. You end up in a loop of “fix this” – and lose track of what actually works.

02

Rejected over small things

No demo account for the reviewer, no way to delete your account, a broken link to the privacy policy. Small slips that cost weeks.

03

The wrong kind of payment

If your mobile app sells digital content through Stripe or Klarna (a popular pay-later service), Apple and Google may say no. Many only find out during review.

04

Rules written for developers

Icon sizes, screenshots, review guidelines and the requirement that your app is more than a wrapped website. This is where many give up.

Questions

Frequently asked questions

More answers to come as we get closer to launch.

Does it work with apps built in Lovable or Bolt?

Yes. That's exactly the kind of app we're building for – from Lovable, Bolt, Cursor and similar tools. You don't need to move or rebuild your app.

What is Store Check?

An early check of your app against the App Store and Google Play requirements, before you submit. Each item comes out green, red or yellow. Yellow means a human needs to make a judgement call, and you get a checklist for it. Examples of checks: that login works and there's a demo account for the reviewer, that users can delete their account in the app, that the privacy policy link works, that there are no broken links, crashes or blank pages, how payments for digital goods are handled, and that icons, screenshots and description are in the right sizes.

Do you guarantee that my app will be approved in the App Store and Google Play?

No. Apple and Google review apps and make the decisions. We lower the risk of rejection by finding common problems before you submit – but we can't promise approval.

What does it mean when a requirement is green?

That the requirement passed the test at that moment. It doesn't mean the app is bug-free. That's why we record every test on video, so you can see for yourself what was tested.

Do you handle my money?

No. Creacom never handles your money or your agreements. Your revenue goes straight to your own Stripe account. Creacom only charges for its own service.

Do I need my own developer accounts?

Yes, if you want to publish in the stores. Your agreements with Apple, Google and Stripe are in your name. An Apple developer account costs $99 a year and a Google one costs a one-time fee of $25. You pay those directly to Apple and Google.

What data do you store?

On the waitlist we only store what you send us yourself: your email, what you've built (if you tell us) and which services you're interested in. This site uses no cookies and no tracking. Once the service is up and running, we only store what the tests need, such as test accounts, and it's stored encrypted. Read more in our privacy policy.

What's the difference between the Test Pack and Monitoring?

The Test Pack is a one-off purchase: up to 20 requirements are tested on video and you get a full Store Check. Monitoring is a subscription that retests everything after each change and on a schedule, and alerts you when something turns red.

Do I need to know how to code?

No. You write requirements in plain language, look at the results and videos, and go through checklists. We take care of the rest.

When does Creacom launch?

We're building it right now and don't have a launch date yet. We're launching in Sweden first. Join the waitlist and you'll be the first to know when you can test your app for free.

Who's behind Creacom?

Creacom is run by Creacom Consulting AB, a Swedish company.
